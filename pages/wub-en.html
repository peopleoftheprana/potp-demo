“I want a daily practice. I just can’t make myself do it alone.”

There’s a reason so many ancient traditions prioritize the early morning hours. It’s quiet, and it’s the perfect time to connect to yourself before you take care of all your other obligations.

I learned that through years of 4:30 a.m. Kundalini practice. When the world went into a collective panic attack in 2020, those mornings kept me calm and grounded, and I started teaching breath on Zoom twice a week to anyone who needed it. Many people later called those classes their lifeline.

In 2021 we moved the classes to 6:30 in the morning and called them Wake Up & Breathe. Some people dropped off. More stayed. A few of the founding members still breathe with me five years later.

Calm is not something you’re born with. It’s something you practice.

Forming a new habit is hard, but it’s way easier when you do it with others. That’s the whole idea: the same time, the same faces, every weekday, until breathing well stops being something you mean to do and becomes the way your day begins.

What kind of breathing practice is this? Where does it come from?

The practices are rooted in the traditions of Kundalini yoga and Sat Nam Rasayan, a meditative healing art. These are time tested practices designed to regulate the nervous system, cultivate inner awareness and support emotional balance. They blend breath, sound, posture and awareness into an accessible, deeply grounding experience. The first 10 minutes are warm up exercises, followed by an 11 minute pranayama (breathing exercise).

Do I need experience with breathwork or yoga?

Nope! Total beginners are absolutely welcome. The sessions are guided step by step, and you can go at your own pace. Whether it’s your first time breathing consciously or you’ve been at it for years, you’ll feel right at home. Some practices may feel challenging at first, but you can adapt them to whatever level you’re at. If you want to learn more about the techniques behind the practices, take Begin with Breath, which comes with two months of Wake Up & Breathe.

What if I can’t make it live?

No problem. Every session is recorded and emailed to you the same day. You also get access to our growing Practice Library, so you can breathe on your own schedule.

What do I need to participate?

Just a quiet space, a comfortable seat and a device with Zoom. You don’t need a mat, special clothing or any fancy tools. Pyjamas are totally fine.

Is this a workout? Will I be sweating?

Not in the traditional sense. Some practices energize you with rhythmic breath patterns, and some can be physically demanding, but the focus is on nervous system regulation, presence and consciousness, not physical exertion. More often than not, it isn’t about physical strength. It’s about developing your mental power.

What are the benefits of practicing regularly?

People report feeling calmer, more focused, better able to handle stress and more connected to themselves and others. The magic is in the consistency: by practicing daily, you give your body and mind a steady foundation of support. Read what our members say.

Is there a community aspect?

Yes! You’re not just breathing alone. On a typical morning there are about ten of us, from about thirty members in all. You’re joining a beautiful, supportive group of people who are committed to personal growth and self care. Over time, the familiar faces and shared intention build a real sense of connection. We’ve been breathing together every weekday since 2021, and some members have been there since the beginning. Those who breathe together, stay together.

What if I miss a few days?

No guilt, no pressure. Life happens. Just come back when you can. The breath will always be there for you, and so will we.

How much does membership cost? Can I try it for free?

Membership is $50 CAD a month plus tax, and your first week is free, so you can try the practices, breathe with us and see if it’s a good fit for you.

Is this the same as the Breathing Room?

No. The Breathing Room is the 15 minute live practice that comes with BEAT + BREATH™, weekdays from 7:00 to 7:15 a.m. Eastern. Wake Up & Breathe is the longer, deeper practice just before it, from 6:30 to 7:00, rooted in Kundalini yoga and Sat Nam Rasayan.

Is breathing safe?

The practices are safe for most people. Please let us know at the start if you have a condition such as high blood pressure, or if you’re pregnant, as there are certain practices we’ll ask you to skip. If a practice brings up more than feels manageable, stop, return to your natural breath and reach out to your care provider. Never practice in water, while driving or while operating machinery.

Is Wake Up & Breathe therapy?

No. It’s breath education and a community practice, created by a psychotherapist but not psychotherapy. It can sit well alongside therapy, and it doesn’t replace care from your own health care provider.
